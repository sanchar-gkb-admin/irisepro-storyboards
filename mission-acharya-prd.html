Mission Acharya — Strengthening Our Teachers

What Mission Acharya is, how it works, and how we are building our platform to support it — and every institutional program that follows — safely, without disturbing anything students use today.
For review · 26 July 2026 · Three streams · Plain-language edition
In one line: Mission Acharya puts our teachers through the same success-abilities journey students already take, and gives each teacher a report designed for educators. Schools bring it in for their staff; our own team enrols the teachers. It is the first offering of a new kind for us — bought by an institution, not by an individual — so we are also laying the groundwork so many more institutional programs can follow without friction.

1What Mission Acharya is

Mission Acharya is our teacher-strengthening program. A teacher goes through the same set of tasks and gets scored on the same 64 success abilities as a student — but the report they receive is written for educators: it highlights the abilities that matter most for good teaching, and measures each teacher against a single national "champion teacher" benchmark.

Everything a teacher does — the tasks, how their answers are scored, the abilities measured — is the same as the student program. Only the report is tuned for teachers. Nothing new has to be invented in how we assess.

2The three Mission Acharya streams

Teachers teach very different age groups, so Mission Acharya has three streams, by the classes a teacher handles:

StreamFor teachers ofWhat's different in the report
1. FoundationPre-KG to Class 4Its own set of 15 priority abilities (early-childhood teaching leans on creativity, patience, adaptability)
2. MiddleClass 5 to 10A different set of 15 priority abilities (analysis, structure, classroom leadership)
3. SeniorPU / UGThe same 15 abilities as Middle, but held to a higher bar — senior teaching demands more

The overall pillar-level benchmark and the graph zones are the same across all three streams. What changes is the list of priority abilities and, for the senior stream, how high the bar is set. This is by design.

3Why this is a new kind of offering for us

Until now, everything on our platform is bought by an individual — a parent or a student — on our public website, and they sign themselves up. Mission Acharya is the first offering that works the other way:

  • A school (an institution) brings it in for its teachers — it is not sold on the public website.
  • Our team enrols the teachers — there is no self-signup and no online payment.

And Mission Acharya is only the first of these. Our institutional page already points to more to come — Colleges, Corporates, CSR programs, Government. So rather than build a one-off, we are creating a proper "institutional lane" that all of these can use. Two clean categories from now on:

5How the report is tuned for teachers

  • One benchmark, in saffron — the "All-India ASLI Champion Benchmark" — instead of the three student benchmarks.
  • 15 priority abilities chosen for educators, specific to each stream (see §2).
  • No career pathways section — that's a student feature.
  • Every teacher is still assessed on all 64 abilities — the 15 are simply what the report puts front and centre.
  • The graphs redraw themselves from the teacher's results — nothing is drawn by hand.

6How the system adapts to support this

In plain terms, here is what we are adding so institutional programs work properly and can grow:

  1. A clear "public vs institutional" label on every offering. The website only ever shows public ones; institutional ones stay admin-only. This label decides everything else automatically.
  2. Team-led enrolment for institutional programs — our admin adds teachers in a batch, with no online-payment step. Choosing the stream automatically marks it institutional; no one has to remember to flag it.
  3. Onboarding that fits the offering — teachers get the teacher guide and skip the student class/guardian questions.
  4. The right report lens, automatically — the system picks the correct teacher benchmark and abilities for the stream a teacher is in.
  5. Built to extend — Corporate, Government, Colleges and others can be added later as more institutional programs in the same lane, without reworking any of this.

7Measures for a seamless rollout — no friction, no defects

This is the first time two categories — public (for individuals) and institutional (for schools) — will live side by side. The whole point of the groundwork is that the new one cannot disturb the old one. Here is exactly how we make sure of that:
  • Students are untouched — and we prove it. Before anything goes live, we regenerate the reports for all 7 existing student programs and check they come out exactly the same as today, letter for letter. If a single number moves, we stop.
  • Institutional programs can never appear on the public website. There is a built-in guard that keeps the public catalogue at exactly its current set, so a new institutional stream can't accidentally show up for sale to the public.
  • We build and test all three streams together, end to end — not one at a time. A half-finished stream can't slip out on its own; the whole thing is proven as one package first.
  • We eyeball a real report for each of the three streams — actually generate a teacher scorecard for Foundation, Middle and Senior and look at each one before switch-on.
  • One switch, and it's reversible. If anything looks off after going live, we can turn it back with no effect on students.
  • Nothing goes live without Goutam's sign-off. The rollout is gated on an explicit go-ahead, after the checks above are green.

9How we'll know it's working

  • A teacher can be enrolled, complete the tasks, and receive the correct teacher report for their stream.
  • The report shows the single saffron benchmark, the right 15 teacher abilities, and no career pathways.
  • The 7 student programs behave and score exactly as before.
  • Nothing about Mission Acharya appears on the public website.

10Timeline — what can start when

Teachers can be enrolled and start their tasks as soon as the streams are switched on. The one thing that must be ready before the first group finishes is the teacher report content — which Sutirtha has now shared for all three streams. After the first cohort finishes, we can fine-tune the benchmarks from real teacher results.
